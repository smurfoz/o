# Scoring Rubric

Score every idea on the same five criteria, 0-5 each (25 total). Score what's
actually in the idea block — don't credit an idea for potential it didn't
state.

| Criterion | 0-1 | 2-3 | 4-5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Demand** — evidence people want this | No signal beyond a guess | Reasonable inference from a known trend or adjacent product | Direct evidence: an existing paying market, a stated pain point, comparable products already selling |
| **Differentiation** — why this beats the obvious alternative | Same as existing options, no edge | A meaningful twist on an existing approach | Hard to copy quickly, or opens a segment competitors are ignoring |
| **Feasibility** — buildable with stated constraints | Needs resources/skills/time the user doesn't have | Doable but stretches budget, team, or timeline | Launchable within the user's stated constraints, first version realistic soon |
| **Monetization** — path to revenue or the stated goal | Unclear how this ever makes money / hits the goal | Plausible path, but pricing or margin is soft | Clear mechanism (price point, unit economics) that plausibly works |
| **Speed to test** — time to first real signal | Needs a full build before you learn anything | A scrappy version is testable in weeks | Testable in days with a landing page, a post, a manual pilot, etc. |

## How to use it

- **Score against the idea block, not against the idea's best-case version.**
  If the block doesn't state a monetization mechanism, that's a low
  Monetization score even if one is plausible in theory — vague ideas should
  score lower until they're sharpened.
- **Weight by what the user actually said they need.** If they explicitly
  want something to validate fast on a shoestring, weight Speed to test and
  Feasibility higher when picking the top recommendation, even if a slower,
  bigger idea scores higher on raw total.
- **Ties:** prefer the idea with the higher Feasibility + Speed to test
  combined score — a good idea you can actually start beats a slightly
  better idea that stays theoretical.
- **Score bands** (rough read on the total, not a hard cutoff):
  - 20-25: strong candidate, worth prioritizing
  - 13-19: viable but has a real gap — name it explicitly
  - 0-12: keep in the list for completeness/contrast, but don't recommend it
    without flagging why it scored low
