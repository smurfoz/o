# Solo UK Marketing Business Ideation

Invent marketing businesses that one person, operating in the UK, can sell
and deliver alone — then rank them on whether they'd actually work, not just
whether they sound good.

## Scope

**A qualifying idea is:**
- A marketing *service* business (an agency-of-one) — not a product, an app,
  or a piece of software.
- Deliverable by one person: no idea should require hiring staff or
  co-founders to run the core loop (subcontracting overflow later is fine;
  needing a team from day one is not).
- Sellable to real UK buyers through a specific, nameable route (not "get
  clients through marketing" — that's circular for a marketing business).

**Do not propose:**
- Slogans, taglines, or naming — that's a deliverable within a business, not
  a business.
- AI products or tools (an AI writing app, an AI ad-generator SaaS, etc.) —
  this is about services sold and delivered by a person, not software built
  and shipped.
- CRM clones or generic "build a CRM/tool for X" ideas.
If a generated idea drifts into any of these, cut it before it reaches the
shortlist rather than scoring it.

## Process

1. **Take the brief as given.** If the user names a niche, budget, existing
   skill, or geography within the UK, treat it as a hard constraint. If
   nothing is specified, default to 6 ideas spanning different marketing
   disciplines (paid media, SEO/content, email/lifecycle, local/offline,
   creative/production, positioning-and-launch) so the set isn't six
   variations on one service type.

2. **Write each idea as a full block** using `idea-block.md`. Every block
   must include a real offer, real unit economics, a specific beachhead, and
   a concrete 90-day path — an idea without all four isn't ready to score.

3. **Score every idea** against `scoring.md` and show the per-criterion
   breakdown, not just the total.

4. **Rank and present:**
   - Summary table: business name, beachhead, total score, one-line verdict.
   - Full blocks in ranked order.
   - Name the top 1-2 picks with a one-sentence "why this one, why now."
   - Flag any high scorer with a hidden dependency worth surfacing (e.g.
     needs a licence, needs an existing network the user hasn't confirmed
     they have).

5. **Offer to go deeper** on one idea (a fuller 90-day plan, a sample outreach
   script, a pricing worksheet) rather than expanding every idea unasked.

## Ground rules

- Unit economics must use realistic UK figures: day/project rates a solo
  operator can credibly charge in that niche, realistic hours per client per
  month, and what's left after tools/subcontracting — not aspirational
  numbers. State assumptions plainly (e.g. "assumes ~£75/hr equivalent,
  8hrs/client/month").
- A beachhead is a specific slice of the UK market (a vertical + company size
  + region/channel), not "UK small businesses." If it can't be described in
  one sentence to a stranger, it's not specific enough.
- The 90-day path must name the first 3-5 real clients come from somewhere
  concrete (a specific outreach channel, an existing relationship, a
  directory/marketplace, a partnership) — "post on LinkedIn and see what
  happens" is not a plan.
- VAT/IR35/limited-company-vs-sole-trader mechanics matter only insofar as
  they affect unit economics or the 90-day path (e.g. staying under the VAT
  threshold changes pricing strategy) — don't pad ideas with generic UK tax
  boilerplate that isn't doing work for that specific idea.
- Don't rescue a weak idea with an inflated score to round out the list — a
  low score is signal, leave it low.
